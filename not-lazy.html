The empty feeling

You're not lazy. Nothing is asking anything of you.

Orion Maxfield · Draft · 6 min read

You finish your work in about thirty minutes. Some days less. You're good at it, and nobody's watching closely enough to notice, so the rest of the day is yours. You already know how it goes.

The same handful of tabs. Something you'd rather not describe to anyone. Around four you look up and the light has changed and you haven't left the apartment or said a word out loud to another person. And somewhere in there the thought arrives, the one that's been arriving for months: what is wrong with me.

So let me say it plainly, because I don't think anyone has said it to you: that isn't laziness. Laziness would mean you didn't want anything. You want things badly. That's exactly why it hurts.

Nobody tells you the scaffolding came down

For the first twenty-odd years of your life, structure showed up whether you asked for it or not. School put you in a room with the same people every day and told you what was due. There were teams, classes, a mission with a companion and a schedule, jobs where someone noticed if your chair was empty. You didn't build any of that. It was just the shape of things.

Then it came down. Not all at once: a move, a graduation, a job that turned into a laptop on a kitchen table, a relationship ending. And nobody announced it. There was no moment where someone sat you down and said: from here on, if you want anyone to expect you anywhere, you have to build that yourself, out of nothing, as an adult, while tired.

So here's where you actually are. You're a capable man with real time and nothing making a claim on it. And a capable man with nothing asking anything of him will find something to fill the hours. The trouble is that the things within easiest reach, the scroll, the tab, the thing at two in the morning, leave you emptier than they found you. Feeling emptier confirms the story that you're failing. And the story makes the next hour harder to face.

That's not a character flaw. It's a loop, and it runs on its own.

Motivation is the wrong tool

You've tried to solve this the way everyone says to. You've made the schedule. You've watched the video about discipline. You've had the Sunday night where it genuinely felt different, and then Wednesday came.

Every time it collapsed, you filed it as more evidence about yourself.

I'd file it differently. The strategy is wrong. Motivation is a terrible fuel for a man who's already running on empty, because motivation runs on how you feel, and how you feel is the thing that's broken right now. You're trying to lift yourself by the part of you that's currently underwater.

What's missing isn't drive. It's obligation to a person.

A goal is a promise you make in private, and you can release yourself from it at eleven at night and nobody will ever know. A person notices.

That's the whole difference. Being expected somewhere gives the day a shape that willpower can't manufacture, and it holds on the days you don't feel like it, which are the only days that actually matter.

One thing, this week

Not a system. Not a morning routine. One standing commitment with two properties: it happens at a fixed time, and there's a human being on the other end who would notice if you didn't come.

A rec league. A class that meets weekly. A volunteer shift where they're counting bodies. A standing call with a friend where you're the one who calls. A calling you actually show up for. It matters much less what it is than that someone is expecting you.

It will feel too small. It is small, next to the size of what you're carrying. Take it anyway.

And I'll be honest about the part I don't fully understand: I don't know exactly why the boring someone-is-waiting-for-me thing works when the ambitious private plan doesn't. My best guess is that it borrows structure from other people until you have enough footing to build your own. That's not a triumphant answer. But I've watched it hold for men it had no business holding for, and I've watched a lot of better-sounding plans fall over by Wednesday.

Start there. The rest of it, the purpose, the direction, the version of your life that actually uses what you're good at, is a real conversation, and it's one I want to have. It just goes better from ground you're standing on.
