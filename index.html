You've been climbing on empty.

Up past midnight, back on the phone. The thing you swore you were done with. Every rule kept and still not enough. A whole week without anyone asking how you actually are.

If you came here at the end of a night like that: you're not the only one, and you're not the worst one. You're tired.

It gets quieter below.

Sit down for a minute.

Everything you've ever read about this told you to climb harder. Get disciplined. Fix the behavior. Try again Monday. You've done that. You've done it for years. It isn't working, and not because you're weak. Because you're doing it on no water.

Here's the picture this whole site is built on. A man is climbing toward everything he's ever wanted, and he hits a wall he can't get over. Off to the side, in the first shade he's seen all day, someone is sitting under a tree. Someone who has plainly made this climb himself. He doesn't tell the man to push harder. He asks him to sit.

That's the whole idea. Not quitting. Sitting. Getting your breath back before you decide anything. The men who make it up that mountain aren't the ones who never stopped. They're the ones who learned when to.
